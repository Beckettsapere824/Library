# Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives — John C. Hull

**Status:** Primary resource for this domain (single entry).

**Author:** John C. Hull  
**Type:** Textbook (industry standard)

## Why this resource

Hull is the default common language of quantitative finance. Interviewers, desks, and risk teams assume its vocabulary: forwards and futures, swaps, option Greeks, Black–Scholes and its extensions, volatility surfaces, interest-rate and credit models, and basic risk management. It is the highest-leverage single starting text for the field’s shared conceptual framework.

It is not a research monograph and not a pure stochastic-calculus course. For continuous-time foundations, pair later with Shreve; for modern machine-learning methods on financial data, pair later with López de Prado. Hull remains the reference that defines the baseline.

## Scope (typical coverage)

- Forwards, futures, swaps
- Options markets and strategies
- Binomial trees and Black–Scholes–Merton
- Greeks and hedging
- Volatility smiles and surfaces
- Interest-rate derivatives and models
- Credit risk and credit derivatives
- Exotic options and other topics depending on edition

Exact chapter set depends on edition. Prefer a recent edition for market conventions and regulatory context.

## How to use

1. Read for shared vocabulary and market mechanics before specialised theory.
2. Work numerical examples; the end-of-chapter problems are useful for interviews and desk intuition.
3. Treat it as a reference after the first pass — return to specific chapters when pricing or risk questions arise.
